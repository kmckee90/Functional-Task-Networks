OPTIONS/JAX OPTION-TRAINING DIAGNOSTIC
======================================
Scope: why the DDPG options (train_options_2.py -> train_on_policy.py) are not
reliably learning to drive the agent to each centroid. I read agent.py, ftn.py,
collect.py, color_world.py, train_options_2.py, train_on_policy.py, config.py and
measured your two existing H=1024 checkpoints (checkpoint.pkl = off-policy only;
checkpoint_on_policy.pkl = after the on-policy loop). I did NOT modify your code.
Eval harness: scratchpad/eval_options.py (greedy rollouts + direction alignment +
critic value/action-gradient probes). Run on GPU 6.


TL;DR (the headline)
--------------------
1. The CRITIC is fine. It learns a correct value function: Q at the goal cell is
   ~0.5 higher than the mean Q elsewhere, in BOTH checkpoints. The reward and the
   Bellman backup are working.

2. The ACTOR is the failure. Off-policy, the options' greedy direction points
   toward their goal with cosine alignment ~+0.02 (i.e. RANDOM). 13/20 options are
   "dead" (adjacent-cell alignment < 0.1). Greedy goal-occupancy is only ~0.15.

3. Your on-policy idea WORKS and is the right instinct. It lifts actor alignment
   from +0.02 -> +0.27 (adjacent +0.03 -> +0.44), good options 4/20 -> 15/20, and
   goal-occupancy 0.15 -> 0.30. It is not broken; it is under-powered/slow.

So this is not "RL isn't learning." It's specifically a DDPG ACTOR problem on an
observation that is harder than it looks, and the off-policy random-walk data is
the wrong distribution to fix the actor with.

How to read these:
- "ever-reach" overstates competence: on a small torus an agent with a non-zero
  but mis-aligned velocity DIFFUSES into the goal cell within H steps by chance.
  That's why off-policy shows 0.67 ever-reach but only 0.15 occupancy and ~0
  alignment. Occupancy and alignment are the honest metrics; ignore ever-reach.
- "critic-ARGMAX alignment" = brute-force best of 32 ring directions under the
  learned critic. It is the BEST the actor could do if it perfectly maximized the
  critic. Off-policy it is only +0.19; on-policy +0.39.


ROOT CAUSE (two compounding failures, both on the actor side)
-------------------------------------------------------------
A. The critic's ACTION-gradient is weak, even though its VALUE function is right.
   Q-gap +0.51 says the critic knows goal cells are worth more (good V). But the
   critic-argmax over actions only aligns +0.19 off-policy: i.e. argmax_a Q(cell,a)
   barely points toward the goal. The actor can be no better than this ceiling.
   Two reasons the action-gradient is weak:
     (i) PARTIAL OBSERVABILITY. obs = the color of the current Voronoi cell only.
         It is piecewise-constant within a cell and encodes NO position. So the
         transition (cell, action) -> next cell is genuinely stochastic in the
         observation: the same (color, a) maps to different next cells depending
         on WHERE in the cell the agent is (hidden). Q(color, a) is therefore an
         average over hidden position, which blurs the dependence of value on the
         action direction. The value function over cells survives the averaging
         (hence good Q-gap); the within-cell action-gradient largely does not.
     (ii) OFF-POLICY ACTION DISTRIBUTION. The random-walk buffer has actions ~
         U(-1,1)^2. The critic is only well-constrained where it has data; a
         deterministic actor then takes argmax_a, which drifts into action regions
         the critic estimated poorly -> the classic DDPG "actor exploits critic
         error on out-of-distribution actions" pathology. Mean |action| ~1.18-1.31
         (tanh saturating toward the corners) is consistent with this.

B. The actor underperforms even the weak critic it has. Off-policy the actor
   aligns +0.02 while the critic's own argmax aligns +0.19 -- the actor is not even
   reaching the (already weak) maximum. DDPG's deterministic policy-gradient gets
   stuck / chases saturated corners. On-policy, the gap closes (actor +0.27 vs
   argmax +0.39): once the critic is accurate AROUND the policy's own actions, the
   policy gradient points somewhere useful.

WHY ON-POLICY HELPS (confirms your hypothesis): collecting under the option's own
epsilon-greedy policy concentrates the action distribution where the actor
actually operates, so the critic's action-gradient there becomes meaningful
(argmax alignment +0.19 -> +0.39) and the actor can follow it (+0.02 -> +0.27).
This is the textbook on-policy benefit for actor-critic under distribution shift.


WHY THE ON-POLICY LOOP IS STILL SLOW / PARTIAL (what's holding it back)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. COLD-START REWARD STARVATION (chicken-and-egg). A fresh option's policy is bad,
   so on-policy episodes from random start poses rarely reach the goal. Your own
   'reach' log printed ~0.01, which is BELOW the goal cell's area fraction (~1/20
   = 0.05): on-policy data from a bad policy visits the goal LESS than random does.
   So early on, pure on-policy collection has fewer reward transitions than the
   random walk it replaced -> the critic/actor get almost no goal signal until the
   policy is already decent. It bootstraps, but slowly, over your 10 epochs.

2. PURE-ON-POLICY DISCARDS THE GOAL-RICH RANDOM DATA. train_on_policy replaces the
   buffer entirely with ~2400 on-policy transitions per option. The random-walk
   buffer (train_options_2) actually has BETTER goal coverage early (~5% in-goal vs
   ~1%). Throwing it away removes the one distribution that reliably contains
   reward. A mixed buffer (random + on-policy) would keep reward coverage while
   adding the near-policy actions the critic needs.

3. TINY COVERAGE. OnPolicyConfig.workers=8, steps_per_option=2400 -> only 8 parallel
   episodes, ~3 episodes each (episode_max_steps=100). That's thin coverage of 20
   cells, and few independent goal approaches per option. On a GPU you can run
   256-1024 parallel rollouts essentially for free (the actor forward is batched).

4. ALL EPISODES START UNIFORMLY OVER THE WHOLE WORLD. No curriculum. From a far
   start a per-cell-constant policy must get many cells' directions right in a row
   to ever see reward. Starting episodes in/near the goal cell first (then widening)
   would give immediate reward signal and bootstrap far faster.

5. done-on-TRUNCATION bias (minor). In both collectors the boundary flag is set on
   timeout, and training uses d = clip(member + boundary); so a timeout transition
   is treated as TERMINAL (target = r, no bootstrap). Time-limit truncation is not a
   true terminal -- this biases values downward near the horizon. Standard fix:
   bootstrap on truncation (only treat goal-reach as terminal). Small effect here
   but free to fix.

6. SHARED-NET INTERFERENCE (secondary). One actor/critic backbone + one linear
   readout shared across 20 options, masks of k=128 in H=1024 (so 20*128=2560 unit-
   slots crammed into 1024 -> heavy overlap). Sequential training lets later options
   perturb earlier ones through shared units/readout rows; the 3-sibling
   consolidation + your 10 epochs partially re-heal this. It is not the primary
   cause (the dead options are not simply the early ones), but it caps quality.


HOW I WOULD TRAIN N OPTIONS TO MAKE THIS SUCCEED (ranked by leverage)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The ceiling problem is the critic's action-gradient under a position-free
observation. The highest-leverage fixes raise that ceiling; the rest help the
actor reach it and speed convergence.

(1) ASYMMETRIC ACTOR-CRITIC (privileged critic). Biggest win, cleanly Markov.
    Give the CRITIC the position (or [pos, color]) while the ACTOR keeps only the
    color obs. You already have pos/next_pos in the buffer (buf[3]/buf[4]) and they
    are NOT used for learning today. With a position-aware critic the transition is
    Markov, Q(pos, a) has a sharp, correct action-gradient, and the deterministic
    policy gradient trains the (color-only) actor properly. This directly fixes
    cause A(i). Common and well-tested for POMDPs.

(2) SPATIAL SHAPING REWARD (dense, privileged). Today option_shaping is forced to
    0.0 because the shaping term uses COLOR distance (meaningless for navigation).
    Replace it with SPATIAL distance from next_pos to the goal centroid's FOOTPRINT
    position (registry.pos[cid], already computed). r = goal*member - beta*||next_pos
    - footprint[cid]||. Even though the policy can't see position, the reward may use
    it; the per-cell MEAN shaped reward decreases with distance to the goal, giving
    a clean cross-cell value gradient and a far stronger learning signal than sparse
    +1. This alone would likely make most options work even off-policy.

(3) TD3-STYLE CRITIC REGULARIZATION. Directly targets cause A(ii)+B:
    - target policy smoothing: add clipped noise to the target action a' before
      computing the target Q (regularizes Q in action space so argmax is sane);
    - twin critics + min (kills the overestimation the actor exploits);
    - delayed actor updates (let the critic settle before the actor chases it).
    These are small additions to agent._train and routinely turn a flaky DDPG into
    a stable one.

(4) MIX RANDOM + ON-POLICY DATA instead of pure on-policy. Keep the train_options_2
    random buffer (goal-rich coverage) and CONCATENATE fresh on-policy transitions
    (near-policy actions). Train on the union. This removes the cold-start reward
    starvation (#1/#2 above) while retaining the on-policy benefit you measured.
    Cheap and likely the single biggest improvement to train_on_policy as written.

(5) CURRICULUM / GOAL-CENTRIC RESETS for on-policy collection. Start a fraction of
    episodes inside or adjacent to the goal cell (reset pose near footprint[cid]),
    then anneal toward uniform. Guarantees reward signal from step 1. (Hindsight
    relabeling -- treat reached cells as goals -- is an alternative that needs no
    privileged info.)

(6) MORE COVERAGE: workers 8 -> 256-1024, and/or larger steps_per_option. Batched
    actor rollouts are cheap on the GPU; this de-noises both collection and the
    occupancy/reach statistics.

(7) FIX done-on-truncation: bootstrap on timeout, terminal only on goal-reach.

(8) Tuning that helps DDPG here: raise critic LR relative to actor (or do multiple
    critic steps per actor step) so the actor chases an accurate critic; consider
    actor_lr/critic_lr 1e-4/3e-4 rather than 1e-4/1e-4; more option_train_steps for
    the off-policy stage.

If I could make only TWO changes: (2) spatial shaping + (4) mixed buffer. If I
could make only ONE structural change: (1) asymmetric position-aware critic.


ANSWERS TO YOUR INLINE QUESTIONS
--------------------------------
Q: Is the agent rewarded for CROSSING into the goal, or for EVERY step it is in the
   centroid's membership?
   The reward expression is per-step membership: r = goal*member - shaping*dist -
   step_cost, with member=1 whenever next_obs's nearest centroid == cid. BUT the
   done flag is d = clip(member + boundary), so being in the goal is treated as
   TERMINAL. In the Bellman target (r + gamma*(1-d)*Q'), d=1 zeroes the bootstrap,
   so each in-goal transition contributes a one-shot terminal +1 rather than an
   accumulating per-step stream. And in the on-policy collector the episode RESETS
   on reaching the goal. Net effect: functionally it is "rewarded for entering the
   goal" (a terminal +1 per entry), NOT "+1 per step accumulated while dwelling."
   (In the random-walk buffer the walk isn't reset, so consecutive in-goal steps do
   appear as separate transitions, but each is independently labeled terminal +1 --
   still no accumulation in the value target.)

Q: In train_on_policy are masks selected deterministically, and are these the same
   masks the demo selects deterministically?
   Yes. Both index the per-centroid actor mask m_a by centroid id out of the same
   options dict loaded from the checkpoint; train_on_policy never reconfigures or
   alters masks (it only updates the shared backbone), so the mask for centroid c is
   identical in training and demo. (Caveat: the demo's --mask-polyak/--mask-delay
   can make the EXECUTED mask a transient blend/lag; with the current defaults
   mask_polyak=0 it fires the exact stored mask.)


WHAT "NOT WORKING" PROBABLY LOOKS LIKE IN YOUR DEMO
--------------------------------------------------
The planner chains one option per hop and needs EACH hop's option to be reliable.
With on-policy occupancy ~0.30 and still 3/20 dead options, any tour whose path
includes a weak/dead option stalls -- consistent with the demo tours that miss.
Raising per-option competence (above) is what unblocks the demo, not changing the
planner.
