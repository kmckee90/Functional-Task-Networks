2. ANATOMY OF ONE DDPG STEP  (why each step costs 69.7 ms at B=1024)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Each scan step processes _TERMS*B = 4*1024 = 4096 rows through the FTN backbone.
Per step it runs, on those 4096 rows:
  - target actor fwd + target critic fwd  (next-state Q)
  - online critic fwd (TD term) + its grad
  - online actor fwd + online critic fwd (actor loss) + grad
  - CQL: cql_n_actions+1 = 11 EXTRA critic forwards (10 random actions, vmapped,
    + 1 policy action) for the logsumexp conservative penalty
  - two manual-Adam updates + two polyak target blends

Measured per-step cost at the real B=1024, H=1024 (cql_diag.py):
  cql_alpha=1.0, cql_n=10  (current) :  69.7 ms/step   -> 15000 steps = 1045 s
  cql_alpha=1.0, cql_n=2             :  29.4 ms/step   -> 15000 steps =  441 s
  cql_alpha=0.0  (pure DDPG)         :  16.0 ms/step   -> 15000 steps =  240 s

==> THE CQL RANDOM-ACTION CRITIC EVALUATIONS ARE 77% OF PER-STEP TIME.
    CQL turns 1 critic forward into ~12, and that 11x extra critic work is the
    single biggest cost in the whole program.

Secondary structural cost: the FTN backbone is a bank of H=1024 independent tiny
MLPs (ftn_layers=8, inner_size=8), evaluated as einsum 'bki,kij->bkj' with the
contracted dims i=j=8. These are thousands of 8x8 matmuls -- they do NOT use the
H100 tensor cores and are launch/bandwidth-bound, not FLOP-bound (consistent with
585 W of 700 W at "100%" util). The H100 is busy but far from its arithmetic peak;
the kernel shape, not raw FLOPs, is the limiter.


3. THE MULTI-GPU QUESTION (and the sequential-training constraint)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The sequential per-option schedule is deliberate (each option capitalizes on the
backbone the earlier options just moved) and must be preserved. That rules out the
obvious "train 20 options on 20 GPUs" -- there is a true data dependency from one
option to the next. The ONLY parallel axis left inside a single run is the per-step
minibatch within one option.

Why naive multi-GPU data-parallelism does NOT help a single run here:
  - Option training is a 15,000-iteration lax.scan of SMALL steps (~70 ms, much of
    it tiny 8x8 matmuls). Sharding the 4096-row batch across GPUs injects a
    gradient all-reduce into EVERY scan step -> 15,000 sequential cross-device
    collectives per option, 300,000 per run. At B=1024 the per-step compute is far
    too small to amortize even NVLink all-reduce latency, so this would likely
    be NEUTRAL-TO-SLOWER, not faster. Many small sequential steps is the worst
    possible shape for multi-GPU.
  - There is no round/epoch parallelism to exploit either: rounds=1, epochs=1.

Net: at the current shape, throwing GPUs at ONE run buys you almost nothing. The
single-run levers are algorithmic/per-step; the multi-GPU lever is across runs.


4. RECOMMENDATIONS, RANKED BY PAYOFF
------------------------------------
A) BIGGEST MULTI-GPU WIN (preserves the sequential design exactly):
   Embarrassingly-parallel FAN-OUT across the 8 H100s -- one full, independent
   train_options_2.py per GPU for seeds / hyperparameter sweeps / ablations.
   A 5.8 h run becomes 8 runs in the same 5.8 h (up to 8x research throughput),
   with ZERO code change beyond CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES per process. Given each run
   is ~6 h and only uses 1 GPU, this is the obvious use of "many GPUs" and the
   thing actually leaving 7 GPUs idle today.

B) BIGGEST SINGLE-RUN WIN (algorithmic, no multi-GPU needed):
   Cut the CQL cost -- it is 77% of per-step time.
     - cql_n_actions 10 -> 2:  per-step 69.7 -> 29.4 ms  => run ~5.8 h -> ~2.5 h (2.4x)
     - cql_alpha 0 (pure DDPG): per-step 69.7 -> 16.0 ms => run ~5.8 h -> ~1.3 h (4.4x)
     - or amortize CQL: apply the conservative penalty every K steps instead of
       every step (keeps most of its regularizing effect at a fraction of cost).
   (These are config/algorithm choices -- flagged because they dominate, not done.)

C) TURN SPARE SINGLE-GPU CAPACITY INTO SPEED -- bigger batch, fewer steps:
   The H100 is launch/bandwidth-bound at B=1024, not saturated. A larger
   batch_size (e.g. 4096) costs well under 4x per step but gives a much better
   gradient per step, so option_train_steps can be reduced for similar learning at
   lower wall-clock. This converts the H100's spare memory/FLOPs into throughput
   on ONE GPU, with no cross-device collectives. (Changes the experiment; flagged
   as a lever, not applied.)

D) ONLY-IF-YOU-INSIST true multi-GPU inside one run (preserves sequentiality):
   Make the per-step batch LARGE (tens of thousands of rows -- e.g. raise
   batch_size and/or fold CQL's action samples into the batch dim), THEN shard
   that batch across the 8 GPUs with jax.sharding/Mesh so per-step compute finally
   exceeds all-reduce latency. This only pays off once the step is big enough; at
   today's B=1024 it is counterproductive. Effectively this is option (C) scaled
   until data-parallelism becomes worthwhile.

E) HOUSEKEEPING (small, free):
   - Enable JAX's persistent compilation cache
     (jax.config.update('jax_compilation_cache_dir', ...)) so the ~5-8 s scan
     compile is paid once across the sweep runs in (A) and across rounds. Marginal
     for one round, real for a fan-out of 8+ runs.
   - ptxas warning (CUDA 12.4 vs >=12.6.3) is benign here; no action needed.


5. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
-------------------
>99.9% of wall clock is 300,000 sequential DDPG steps; within a step, CQL's 10
random-action critic evals are 77% of the cost. Because the work is many tiny
sequential steps, more GPUs do NOT speed a single run -- use them to fan out
seeds/sweeps (up to 8x), and speed a single run by cutting cql_n_actions and/or
growing the batch while shrinking the step count.
