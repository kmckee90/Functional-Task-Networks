FEEDBACK ON THE FUNDING PROPOSAL
Based on draft.txt dated October 12, 2026

Overall assessment

Keep the ambition. For this investor, the proposal should make the reader imagine an intelligence that develops through its own life: discovering the world, acquiring abilities, retaining them, and using them to make discoveries it could not previously reach. That is a compelling moonshot.

The current draft spends too much of its opening explaining why existing approaches are inadequate, before making that possibility vivid. It also sometimes presents the destination as if the mechanisms already establish that we can reach it. A stronger pitch would distinguish three things throughout: the future you want to create, the mechanisms you have built, and the next experiment that could validate or challenge the connection between them.

This feedback is an editorial and research-framing review, not a comprehensive literature audit. Claims of priority, comparisons with other methods, costs, and biological correspondence need supporting evidence in the linked reports.


1. Lead with the kind of intelligence you want to bring into existence

Your most motivating idea is currently distributed across several sections. Put it in the first paragraph: an agent whose abilities accumulate through experience, with each acquired ability making further learning possible.

Possible title:
    An Intelligence That Grows Through Experience
    Autonomous exploration and lifelong learning toward naturalistic AGI

Possible opening, offered as a starting point rather than a replacement for your voice:

    Imagine an artificial agent placed in an unfamiliar world with no catalog of tasks and no instructions for every skill it will need. It explores, discovers what it can do, and retains what it learns. A discovery becomes an ability; that ability opens the way to another discovery. Over time, it can attempt things that were beyond its reach at the beginning.

    I want to build this process of cumulative development into an artificial intelligence. The goal is an agent that becomes more capable through its own experience, with progressively less dependence on people to choose its lessons. I have developed two ingredients for this effort: a method for learning how to explore, and a neural architecture for retaining and recovering different learned abilities. This proposal funds the next test: whether these ingredients can sustain a growing repertoire of useful behaviors over a long lifetime.

Then show a short video and explain what it demonstrates. The investor should encounter the possibility and the working research before encountering an extended critique of the field.


2. Make the central hypothesis explicit

The proposal needs one sentence connecting your two contributions to the moonshot:

    My hypothesis is that an agent can sustain its own development if it learns both how to find useful new experience and how to preserve and recombine the abilities that experience creates.

Then distinguish the roles:

    M2E: learn where and how to seek unfamiliar experience, using feedback about what has already been encountered.

    FTN: retain different learned solutions in overlapping subnetworks and recover an appropriate solution from examples of the current situation.

    Their proposed combination: turn discoveries into reusable capabilities, then use those capabilities to reach new opportunities for discovery.

Explain an overlapping subnetwork once in plain language: different abilities can use different selections of shared neural resources, instead of requiring every situation to overwrite the same solution.

Do not introduce ANN, RL, meta-learning, cortical columns, LoRA, and attractor dynamics before the reader understands this division of labor. Those details belong behind descriptive links.


3. Define what would count as cumulative capability

This is the most important scientific and narrative distinction in the proposal. Visiting more ground, finding unfamiliar images, learning to predict them, and acquiring useful abilities are different accomplishments.

A concrete example can carry the entire argument:

    First the agent discovers water. Then it learns to reach water reliably. Later it uses that ability to support longer journeys. Eventually it combines navigation, resource gathering, and other learned behaviors to accomplish something it could not do before.

Label this as a desired developmental sequence, not an observed result unless you can demonstrate it.

Your next experiments should show four things separately:
    Discovery: does the agent continue finding new situations or interactions?
    Acquisition: can it deliberately reproduce a useful outcome?
    Retention: can it still reproduce that outcome after learning other things?
    Composition: can earlier abilities be combined to achieve a new outcome?

These give the investor a way to judge progress toward the large vision without requiring you to promise AGI on a schedule. They also clarify why FTN matters: a growing collection of retained, usable capabilities is a more direct test of its value than a visually interesting mask animation.


4. Separate what exists from what is proposed

Include a small, prominent status table or three short blocks:

    Built: the exploration and continual-learning components, with links to the specific demonstrations that support their capabilities.

    Running now: long-lived Globo experiments comparing exploration approaches, recording spatial discovery, achievements, and world-model behavior.

    Next: using FTN to retain goal-reaching behaviors, selecting among them, and testing whether this supports cumulative exploration and increasingly complex behavior.

The recent option/hierarchy idea is especially useful here, but it is a proposed next step. Avoid implying that the existing combined agent already has a growing library of reusable skills or recursive planning.

Similarly, the current FTN integration into Dreamer's world model is an experiment with a particular placement of FTN. It does not establish that this is its most useful placement. Present that architectural flexibility as a reasoned research choice: the next test puts retention directly into behavior, where its contribution can be measured.

Archive preliminary results as Phase 0 and label them accordingly. Any quantitative comparison should state the seed, observation budget, model configuration, and metric. Our current Dreamer+Novelty and P2E configurations differ in model size, so their curves are not a controlled test of exploration objective alone. Early spatial coverage is evidence of exploration behavior, not evidence of AGI or general superiority.


5. Replace the broad attack on current AI with a precise unmet need

The reader does not need to believe that LLMs or the whole continual-learning literature are misguided to believe this project is valuable. The strongest case is that the particular developmental loop you want has not been demonstrated by your current system and deserves a focused attempt.

Suggested framing:

    Today's impressive AI capabilities do not, by themselves, give us an agent that continually decides what to learn from its own life, retains that learning, and builds on it. This project targets that developmental process directly.

Specific claims to revise or support:

    “Updating their network weights ... costs tens to hundreds of millions” conflates different kinds of training and updating. Specify large-scale pretraining if that is what you mean; do not imply every weight update has that cost.

    “The data are invariably human artifacts” is too absolute. Focus instead on the dependence you aim to reduce: people curating experiences, objectives, and training stages.

    “The most well known ... algorithms only target domain shift, require task labels” bundles several different limitations into a blanket claim. Put a named comparison in the technical report, with exact assumptions for each method.

    “Most methods predictably fail” and “Most also reduce to ... random actions” need a defined set of methods and evidence. They are particularly awkward alongside your use of model-based exploration methods that learn policies. Explain the limitation you test, rather than dismissing exploration research wholesale.

    Whether EWC or Go-Explore appears in major products does not establish whether your approach works. Remove that argument and the speculation that your methods will become well known. Use the space for your strongest demonstration.

    “Assumption-free,” “unbounded implications,” and “no constraints” invite easy objections. State the specific freedoms you provide, such as not requiring supplied task identities in the tested FTN setting, and name the remaining assumptions in the linked report.

These changes preserve the moonshot while making its technical foundation harder to dismiss.


6. Explain the novelty problem honestly

“Maximize novelty” is easy to understand, but it immediately raises a good question: why won't the agent stare at noise, seek arbitrary surprises, or keep finding new scenery without learning anything useful?

Address this in the main narrative, briefly:

    The challenge is to turn unfamiliar experience into useful ability. We will measure whether discoveries become behaviors the agent can reproduce, retain, and build upon—not merely whether its inputs keep changing.

The linked M2E report can explain the distinction between density feedback as policy input and novelty as a training objective, along with tests involving noise, aliasing, and repeated observations. Specify what “immediate progress” means. An entirely unstructured random environment cannot support arbitrary learned regularities; the motivating claim is that the agent should exploit the learnable structure that exists without depending on an already accurate world model.


7. Give the neuroscience connection its own evidential ladder

Separate biological inspiration, computational mechanism, and biological prediction.

    Inspiration: observations about distributed activity, overlapping representations, and cortical organization motivate FTN.

    Mechanism: selected neural resources support different learned functions, with a procedure for recovering or adapting their selection.

    Prediction: identify a measurable consequence that could distinguish the account from alternatives in neural or behavioral data.

The current spatial mask display needs particular care. Its permutation algorithm deliberately places co-active atoms near one another for visualization; it does not rearrange the model's actual computation. Therefore, compact-looking regions in that display are not independent evidence that cortical columns emerged. They can illustrate learned overlap and co-activation, but the proposal should say how the display is constructed.

“Testable theory of continual learning in the neocortex” becomes much stronger if followed by one concrete prospective test. Until then, “a computational hypothesis inspired by cortical organization” is a more defensible description.


8. Make the funding request as concrete as the vision is ambitious

At present, the draft does not yet tell the reader what decision to make. Add the amount sought, duration, people, compute needs, and the experiment that funding buys. If the terms are undecided, make those explicit placeholders rather than hiding the ask behind a link.

Suggested milestone sequence, to attach to a realistic budget and timeline:

    A. Establish repeatable exploration baselines across several environments and seeds, with clear diagnoses of stagnation.

    B. Show retained goal-reaching capabilities: learning new behaviors without losing earlier ones, with matched alternatives that do not use FTN.

    C. Show cumulative benefit: retained behaviors make later discoveries faster or enable outcomes inaccessible to the flat explorer under a comparable budget.

    D. Test transfer to unfamiliar conditions; proceed toward robotics only when the simulated capabilities justify the next experiment.

For each milestone, specify the observable result and the decision it informs. A particularly useful failure criterion is: if stored behaviors cannot be reliably reactivated, or do not improve subsequent learning relative to a matched baseline, the proposed loop has not yet been demonstrated.

Also give the investor a short “why me, why now” paragraph: what you personally built, what evidence exists, what infrastructure is ready, and what support would accelerate. State whether this is philanthropic research support, sponsored research, or an investment with a proposed commercialization structure. If relevant, clarify ownership and institutional arrangements without assuming them.


9. Keep the distant consequences, but write them as possibilities

The science-fiction-scale ambition is an asset for this audience. An intelligence that develops its own experimental competence, accumulates practical knowledge, and eventually participates in discovery is worth describing.

The logical bridge should remain visible: retained skills could enable longer experiments; experiments could enable causal learning; those capabilities could support more sophisticated invention and inquiry. None of those transitions follows automatically from a novelty reward.

Replace “The agent will be capable of ... invent technologies” with a conditional vision such as:

    If this developmental loop can scale, its long-term significance extends beyond mastering a game. It could support machines that learn unfamiliar physical tasks and, eventually, formulate and test their own hypotheses. That is the larger possibility this program is designed to investigate.

Make robotics a demanding transfer milestone, rather than an automatic consequence of success in simulated worlds. Potentially valuable intermediate outcomes—retained adaptive skills, efficient exploration, or reusable learning components—can give the program value before its AGI ambition is resolved.


10. Rewrite the safety section around experimental control

Remove the criticism of frontier labs and the claim that grounding necessarily makes this agent more trustworthy. First-person experience and a specified intrinsic drive do not, on their own, establish reliability or alignment. “Fully responsible for its own capabilities” and “perpetual self-perfection” also imply guarantees the proposal has not earned.

A stronger short paragraph would say what you will actually control: experiments begin in isolated simulated environments, capabilities and failure modes are logged, expansion into external tools or physical systems requires a separate evaluation, and runs can be stopped and restored from checkpoints. Distinguish controls already implemented from those you commit to adding.

Finite compute is a resource boundary, not a complete safety argument. Monitoring is useful only to the extent that the signals and responses are specified. The linked safety report can develop those details as the agent acquires new abilities. This can remain a brief, practical section without displacing the motivating vision.


11. Use HTML to create a short main story with optional depth

Suggested main-page order:

    The vision: intelligence that develops through experience.
    The demonstration: a brief annotated clip and an honest statement of what it shows.
    The missing loop: discovery -> retained ability -> further discovery.
    The two contributions: M2E and FTN, explained in plain language.
    The decisive next experiment: retained behaviors and cumulative capability.
    The funding request: resources, milestones, and reporting.
    The larger possibilities: robotics, scientific discovery, and neuroscience.
    Experimental boundaries and safety.

Aim for a main narrative that takes roughly five minutes to read. It must explain the proposal and the ask without requiring any link to be opened. Let the linked reports carry implementation detail, literature comparisons, benchmark definitions, full curves, and limitations.

Replace every repeated “read more” with a link that advertises its value:
    Watch the agent explore an unfamiliar world
    How novelty feedback changes exploration
    How FTN retains and recovers different learned functions
    Preliminary results: Phase 0 experiments
    The next experiment: discoveries becoming reusable skills
    Milestones, budget, and decision points

Use one simple diagram of the developmental loop, one strong demonstration, and one intelligible results figure on the main page. More plots belong in the reports. For demos, provide captions with the actual observation step, whether footage is accelerated, the actions available, and whether the behavior was autonomously learned. Explain reconstruction and prediction panels before showing them to a new reader. A static fallback image helps when video is slow or unavailable.

Investor-facing links must be hosted independently of the laptop's localhost tunnel. Publish frozen, dated evidence for the proposal; optionally offer the live monitor as a separate view. That keeps the proposal readable when runs stop or the research system changes.


12. Final editorial pass

Choose one name and abbreviation for the exploration method across the proposal, plots, and demos. The draft uses M2E; the current monitor says MetaLearning2Explore (M2L). That mismatch creates unnecessary confusion.

Use shorter paragraphs and active, concrete verbs. Replace repeated abstractions such as “generality,” “native capabilities,” and “emergent solutions” with an example of what the agent does or what an experiment measures. Describe emergence as an outcome to investigate, not a substitute for explaining the mechanism.

Correct “thes,” “reward fuction,” “princple,” “and and,” “aspects training,” “skillfullly,” and “datasets to every possible motor skill.” These are minor, but they will matter once the document is presented as a funding request.

The strongest closing returns to the experiment and the ambition together:

    The immediate goal is a convincing demonstration of an agent whose acquired abilities make further learning possible. The larger ambition is to discover whether that process can become a foundation for intelligence that develops through its own life.
