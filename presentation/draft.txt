Brain-Inspired Autonomous Exploration and Learning for Self-Improving AGI  

# Summary
    Real, self-scaling Artificial General Intelligence requires a fundamental shift in approach that starts with autonomous learning.
    Mastery of an environment requires deeply exploring the scope and mechanics of that environment and translating them into robustly retained knowledge and skills.
    Here, I will propose, design, and even demonstrate such an autonomous agent with emergent, native capabilities arising from a rich first-person experience.
    I will demonstrate techniques for highly efficient, naturalistic exploration and neocortex-inspired continual learning, both developed here at the Astera Institute to
    specifically target this vision of AGI.
    Together, thes techniques will form the fundamental ingredients of a self-improving agent.    

# General problems with ML and RL
    Current state of the art AI relies on artifical neural networks (ANNs), and hence suffer from unsolved problems with ANNs generally.
    ANNs provide general learning from data, but cannot retain their solutions unless the data distribution is always the same throughout training.
    Reinforcement learning provides a general method of long-range credit assignment and planning, but only when a reward function is well defined and easily observed.
    Robust AGI can expect none of these luxuries: the world constantly changes, and reward functions are just an experimental way that we inject our own knowledge.
    To effectively leverage ANNs for autonomous AGI, a method of continual learning is needed that can automatically make the numerous decisions involved in neural resource allocation, protection, and updating.
    To exploit the generality of RL, the reward fuction must be internally generated to drive an intrinsic self-improvement loop.

# Problem with current AI
    Agentic Large Language Models (LLMs) are not truly autonomous.
    Updating their network weights at-scale costs tens to hundreds of millions of dollars in data and computation.
    The data are invariably human artifacts ranging from web scraping to remote teleoperations.
    LLM training is therefore limited to these artifacts and to learning a flattened projection of the real intelligence that led to their creation.
    
# Inadequacy of continual learning methods
    Existing continual learning methods do not have the required autonomy or generality for AGI.
    The most well known, general-purpose continual learning algorithms only target domain shift, require task labels, and involve other manual interventions. [link: read more]
    Furthermore, they do not reflect or exploit what is known about cortical organization, which offers deep hints into the preservation of memories and skills over a lifetime. [link: read more]

# Inadequacy in exploration methods
    Exploration methods similarly all target specific aspects training and leave major domains unaddressed. [link: read more]
    No one has found a combination of exploration drives and architectures that results in reliable self-improvement over arbitrary problem domains.
    Most methods predictably fail when deployed to randomized or noisy environments. 
    Most also reduce to some structured placement of random actions instead of allowing the agent to learn more intelligent ways to explore over time.
    Natural, human-like exploration is a progression of increasingly sophisticated exploitations of environmental and control regularities,
    culminating in hypothesis testing and scientific reasoning.

# Solutions for AGI
    At Astera, I have found novel solutions to both of these problems: (1) a meta-learning-based exploration (M2E) and (2) cortex-inspired continual learning, called functional task networks (FTN), 
    specifically to target Astera's goals for naturalistic, brain-like AGI.    

    M2E introduces a concise princple with far-reaching implications for intelligence: 
    Learn behavior that maximizes novelty by remembering and reasoning about past behavior and past novelty.
    When the environment is random or intractable, M2E leverages its internal compasses to make immediate progress.
    [link: read more]

    To solve continual learning, FTN uses insights about cortical connectivity, cellular complexity, population firing statistics, and evidence for meta-stable attractor dynamics. 
    How does the brain perform continual learning so effortlessly, and what if we shaped our neural networks like cortical tissue?
    What if each cell were a complex, trainable unit, and populations of such cells rapidly self-organized to meet the needs of each situation? 
    FTN uses a single optimization inner-loop to drive the autonomous recombination of units into subnetworks that implement previously trained skills and representations,
    and also to synthesize new networks on new task domains. 
    [link: read more]

    Both M2E and FTN were designed to be assumption-free and and "hands off": in M2E, the environment may be predictable or random, continuous or discrete, repetitious or not.
    With FTN, new tasks can flow through the network without any identifying labels, may represent contradictory solutions, or may represent complementary solutions over disjoint spaces.
    There are also no constraints on how either method is applied to ANNs generally.
    Generative modeling -- transformers, diffusion, all state-of-the-art techniques are applicable.

# Plan for AGI
    I will deploy a prototype AGI with exploration and continual learning ability to naturalistic, open-world environments (Globo, Minecraft, real robots).
    The agents will run for long lifetimes and accumulate knowledge and innovations as a result of their own internal drive toward discovery.
    The central innovation of this project will be perfecting the feedback loop between exploration and learning that causes flexible representation and reasoning to naturally emerge.
    The agent will tentatively use state-of-the-art world modeling, planning, and reasoning where applicable, but will avoid becoming bottlenecked on the specific limitations
    of those existing techniques in favor of emergent, autonomous, and brain-like neural solutions.

# Contributions to Technology
    This proposal concerns a form of intelligent, self-improving autonomy that is simply not present in current technology. 
    Successful demonstration of this AGI approach itself will represent a leap in capabilities and opportunities.
    If the agent can master natural open-world environments, then it can be extended to robotics, eventually eliminating the need for massive, 
    contrived human datasets to every possible motor skill.  
    We can also revise the role of LLMs in broader agentic systems, grounding language representation in multimodal first-person experience,
    motor control, and self-modeling.

    Besides the innovation itself, an autonomous AGI of this type can grow to participate in, contribute to, and change society in ways that humans cannot alone.
    The agent will be capable of pursuing its own intrinsic scientific goals, invent technologies, and propose theories that are not just
    statistical interpolations of existing literature.
    Rather, science and engineering are the natural extensions of its fundamental exploration and learning design.
    

# Contributions to Neuroscience
    Besides its centrality to this AGI design, FTN is based closely on several brain mechanisms and serves as a testable theory
    of continual learning in the neocortex. 
    FTN provides a principle for network self-assembly at the levels of dendrites, neurons, and cortical columns.
    The FTN-LoRA architecture in particular resembles dendritic redundancy and selection.
    Spatial consolidation in FTN results in "mesoscale functional units" or even cortical columns, strongly resembling those seen
    in mammals corresponding to different skilled motor behaviors.
  
    M2E stands to contribute to our theories of human curiosity and exploration by demonstrating extremely fast
    exploration progress and the open-ended learning of exploration skills.
    When humans do not know what to do in novel situations, we do not begin taking random actions and we do not probe 
    for every possible uninteresting fact. 
    Rather, we take routes that provide the most useful information that allows us to learn the fastest.
    This is the general intuition behind M2E: not just training the network to do the next novel thing,
    but training it to skillfullly seek out the most efficient routes to the largest discoveries.


# Safety
    Any AGI design presents major safety concerns for society that we must plan for as far in advance as possible.
    Most failures of AI safety are already being speedily pioneered by the frontier labs. 
    This version of AGI, if successful, would have both pros and cons with respect to the usual issues:
    
    For instance, grounding in first-person experience with well-understood internal drives will create a more interpretable,
    trustworthy system than current LLMs, which anarchically imitate contradictory projections of human values and behavior.
    Application of a grounded AGI would also be less subject to the incomplete, post-hoc bridging of modalities and control systems currently
    characteristic of LLM agents.
    Rather, our agent would be fully responsible for its own capabilities and exist in a perpetual self-perfection loop that will take
    their reliability beyond what we can do by intervening.
    
    However, alignment issues remain a standard problem for autonomously self-trained agents. 
    This program will abide by better standards of experimental design than currently exhibited by frontier labs
    by keeping the agent in closed environments where its capabilities are strictly circumscribed.
    Experiments will use intensive, active monitoring and pausing to detect and prevent concerning outcomes.
    For the foreseeable future, hardware will remain an obvious limitation on agent scope and capabilities, and so
    we will design experiments with strict, finite computing resources. 

    Further safety issues must be anticipated and addressed concretely as the prototyping progresses.
    The current phase of development leaves them largely speculative.
    I will elaborate on safety strategies in later proposals for the continuation of the research long-term.
